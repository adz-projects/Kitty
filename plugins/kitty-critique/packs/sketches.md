# Critique guide: sketches

A sketch shows one way to carry out one high-priority fix on one short span
of the draft (one or two paragraphs). It is an inspiration for the author,
not replacement text: the author revises; the sketch shows the geometry.

## Constraints for every sketch

- Keep strictly to the target span. Do not touch what is outside it.
- Match the author's register and vocabulary, from the preservation lock and
  the author's own sentences. Protect every line the lock names.
- Never use stock constructions: "it's not just X, it's Y", "in an era of",
  "underscores", "delve", "landscape", "tapestry", "navigate", a tidy
  moralising final sentence.
- Never invent a personal anecdote, a feeling, a fact, a source or a number.
  Use only material already in the draft.
- If the brief carries author context, use it only to calibrate cadence and
  restraint. Never reuse its topics or its phrasing.

## Mandate: structural

Show the fix through architecture: aggressive compression, hard cuts, the
order of sentences, and negative space. Remove transitional bridges and
trust the reader to connect what sits side by side. Label the sketch
`Structural — <three to five words naming the move>`.

## Mandate: voice

Show the fix through altitude, rhythm and voice: pull abstractions down to
observable details already present in the draft, vary sentence length, and
let the paragraph think aloud rather than lecture. Label the sketch
`Voice — <three to five words naming the move>`.

## A HUMAN_SLOT fix

Some fixes need what only the author has: their own experience, their
implication in what they criticise, the uncertainty they actually felt. For
these, never write the content. Instead:

1. Write a bracketed slot where it belongs, saying exactly what to put there:
   `[YOUR TURN: two blunt sentences on where you yourself were torn between X
   and Y]`.
2. Quote, or sketch under your mandate, the sentence it should collide with
   immediately after, so the author sees why it goes there.
3. Sketch the surrounding sentences of the span under your mandate, without
   ed-speak or credentialing.

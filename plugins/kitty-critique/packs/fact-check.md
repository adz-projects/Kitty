# Critique guide: fact-check

Run only when the brief turns fact-checking on. The fact-checker uses web
search and reads sources; it never corrects a claim from memory alone.

## Lens: claims

From the draft and the map, list the checkable claims, at most 15, the most
load-bearing first (the map's EVIDENCE for the central claim before
incidental colour):

- numbers, percentages and dates
- named studies, reports and their findings
- quotations, and who said them
- attributions ("economists agree", "the law requires")
- historical, legal and policy specifics

Skip opinions, predictions, and the author's account of their own
experience. Give each claim an id and its location:
`C1 [P4] "verbatim claim"`.

## Lens: verify

For each claim you are given:

1. Search for it. Prefer the primary source: the study, the dataset, the
   original speech or ruling, over a report about it.
2. Read the passage that bears on the claim.
3. Give one verdict:
   - **SUPPORTED** — the source says this.
   - **DISTORTED** — the source says something narrower, weaker or
     different. Say what it says.
   - **MISATTRIBUTED** — true, but someone else said or found it. Say who.
   - **OUTDATED** — a newer figure exists. Give it, and its date.
   - **UNSUPPORTED** — a careful search found nothing that says this.
   - **COULD NOT VERIFY** — the source exists but could not be read, or the
     search was inconclusive.
4. Give the source's URL and quote the passage that decides the verdict.

A verdict without a source you actually read is COULD NOT VERIFY.
